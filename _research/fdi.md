Abstract: This paper studies the potential productivity spillover when firms with low produc-
tivity are in close proximity to firms with high productivity. Using a firm-level dataset
from Viet Nam, I examined the spillover effect of foreign investment firms on domestic
Vietnamese firms, which are relatively smaller and have lower valueadded per person.
The exposure is measured in CES form, weighed by spatial proximity and total factor
productivity of revenue TFPR, which resulted in a strong positive correlation. Building
on the Hopenhayn (1992) model of firm entry and exit, I modeled an economy with differ-
ent firm types of different log-normal distributions. In this model, low-productivity type
learns from the highest type by exposure, which then yields a unique equilibrium. The
model calibration shows the trade-off between the low-productivity type’s spillover bene-
fits and its market share loss, suggesting an optimal FDI exposure to maximize domestic
production in the case of the Vietnamese market.
